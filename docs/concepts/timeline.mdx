---
title: The timeline
description: How sequences, tracks, clips, and linked audio are modeled, and what determines layer order.
---

A composition owns one timeline: an ordered stack of tracks holding media or generated clips.
Projects may define multiple compositions; `cinesim.toml` selects the active one. Ordinary clips on
a track reject overlaps.

## Tracks

Tracks come in three kinds, and a track's kind decides what can be placed on it.

| Kind    | Holds                      | Stack  |
| ------- | -------------------------- | ------ |
| Video   | Video and image components | Visual |
| Overlay | Visual layers above video  | Visual |
| Audio   | Audio components           | Audio  |

Audio-only assets can go only on audio tracks. Video and image components can go on either video or
overlay tracks. Placements that break these rules are rejected rather than silently coerced.

### Layer order is authored state

Track order is authored source state. Index `0` is the
uppermost track, and upper visual tracks composite over lower ones. That order survives save and
load, and it matches what you see in the UI.

New tracks enter where you would expect: video and overlay tracks at the top of the visual stack,
audio tracks at the bottom of the audio stack. Reordering takes a zero-based destination index.

You can rename, mute, lock, reorder, add, and remove tracks. Removal is deliberately conservative —
a track must be unlocked and empty first, so you cannot lose clips to a misclick.

## Clips

Every clip declares whether it is `video` or `audio`. Nothing infers media kind at playback time
from context.

A clip has a position on the timeline, a source range within its asset, and a transform. Each clip
carries a stable ID that does not change as you edit around it, which is what lets the CLI and
agents refer to a specific clip reliably.

### Linked audio

Adding a video asset that has sound creates two clips at once — a video clip and an audio clip that
reference each other. Cinesim picks an available audio track or creates one.

Linked clips behave as a unit. Moving, trimming, splitting, or deleting either one applies to both,
as a single edit and a single undo step. Explicit link and unlink commands are available through
the semantic command pathway.

### Fades

Clips can carry linear fade-in and fade-out envelopes — opacity for video, gain for audio. Combined
fades cannot exceed the clip's duration.

Editing around a fade keeps it coherent: trimming clamps existing fades to the new length, and
splitting keeps the outer fades while clearing the two new interior edges.

## Timing

All semantic timing is integer microseconds. Source helpers such as `seconds()` and `milliseconds()`
lower deterministically, which is why the same edit produces the same result in the desktop app,
the CLI, and MCP tools.

When you type times into the CLI you can use friendlier forms — `4.2s`, `250ms`, `3m`, `01:23.500` —
and they are converted to microseconds at the boundary.
